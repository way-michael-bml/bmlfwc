Community Risk Assessment

Observations and recommendations from our neighborhood walk-through

← Back to Documents

These observations and recommendations were prepared by the Nevada County Fire Safe Council following a walk-through of our neighborhood, as part of Phase 2 of our recognition milestones. They’ll inform the full Community Risk Assessment and 3-year Action Plan. Jump to the recommendations.

Observations

Parcels & Structures

  • The community includes 72 parcels, 16 of which are undeveloped. Three parcels make up the Banner Mountain Lookout and its associated development.
  • A few parcels have more than one home, and there are many outbuildings. One parcel has an occupied mobile home in addition to its main house.
  • Most structures have combustible siding and asphalt shingle roofs. All appear to have metal gutters.

Vegetation & Fuels

  • Fire impact to the neighborhood will likely be driven mostly by vegetation. Forest cover is mixed conifer — primarily ponderosa pine, Douglas-fir, and incense cedar — with a mix of hardwoods.
  • Many residents have already completed commendable vegetation hazard reduction, but most parcels would benefit from additional work, and dense vegetation borders most of the community.
  • Some homes still have vegetation in the 0–5 ft. zone and in the remaining zones out to 100 ft.
  • On some larger parcels, heavier vegetation beyond 100 ft. could threaten homes on those and neighboring parcels in a wind-driven fire.
  • Roadside vegetation along several streets — some of it heavy — is a potential threat during evacuation.

Surrounding Lands

  • Banner Mountain Lookout is surrounded by other forested communities with varying levels of vegetation removal and housing density.
  • A large block of forestland owned by the city of Nevada City borders the neighborhood and has had little or no hazard-reduction vegetation removal.
  • These adjacent lands represent a potential threat to the community in a wildfire event.

Access & Evacuation

  • The entire community is tributary to Banner Lava Cap Road, with some parcels having direct driveway access to it.
  • Evacuation would route to Banner Lava Cap Road and then either west or east; once there, evacuation options are still limited.
  • Many streets in the community are narrow, but most can handle two-way traffic.

Community Organization

  • The community has a good start on organizing, with a communication network in place via email, phone/text, and this website.

Recommendations

Based on these observations, the Nevada County Fire Safe Council recommends:

  • Make evacuation planning a high priority, given the heavy vegetation on Banner Mountain and in patches within the community, and the heavy traffic likely to crowd our limited evacuation routes in an emergency.
  • Encourage more hazard-reduction vegetation removal, particularly along roads and in the 0–5 ft. zone around structures. Strive to complete treatments for at least 50 ft. on both sides of all roads.
  • Encourage homeowners to request Defensible Space Advisory Visits, which explain appropriate measures to reduce risk to both structures and vegetation. Consider having at least one community member trained as a Defensible Space Advisor.
  • Keep expanding community communication to share information and successes. Encourage residents to download the Watch Duty app and sign up for the Ready Nevada County alert system (see Evacuation & Alerts).
  • Work with Nevada City to explore cooperating on management of the large forest ownership north of the community, which could include jointly applying for hazard-reduction grants.
  • Watch for grant opportunities to help with community Firewise work.
  • Work with Friends of Banner Mountain to help make all of Banner Mountain safer from the effects of wildfire.
  • Hold annual meetings, which are required for all Firewise communities (more can be held). Use them to discuss fire-safety ideas, improvements happening across the Banner Mountain area, and recommendations from fire organizations, and to share ideas among neighbors. Consider inviting someone from the Nevada County Consolidated Fire District.
  • Consult the Nevada County Consolidated Fire District about whether roads in the community are suitable for emergency equipment, and whether they have maps showing this. Such maps are an important reference for out-of-area crews during an emergency.
